# Extended abstract

**Status:** Manuscript in preparation (unpublished).

Gate-defined bilayer-graphene quantum dots are limited by charge noise. We asked whether AC-modulated confinement creates a dynamical sweet spot that extends T2* relative to static confinement. Using a 4-band McCann–Koshino k·p model in a plane-wave basis (validated by five structural anchors and later cross-checked against an atomistic tight-binding solver, E0 = 60.317 vs 60.32 meV and Δ01 = 0.989 vs 0.989 meV), a period-averaging treatment first suggested a T2* gain of 1.0926× at A_AC = 50 meV. Verification refuted this: the Δ01(Δ_ext) curve is convex at the operating point (∂²Δ01/∂Δ_ext² ≈ +0.48), and a full-Hilbert Floquet calculation shows 37.6–95.2 % leakage out of the qubit subspace per period for A_AC = 20–50 meV, invalidating the period-averaging approximation where the gain appeared; where leakage is small the effect is negligible. Six tuned sweet-spot candidates all dissolved under convergence tests, whereas exact-symmetry protections (mirror-symmetric double-well detuning; valley-diagonal spin qubit) survived. We conclude that, within the envelope model, charge-noise protection in BLG dots is symmetry-locked rather than tunable, and that the limiting intervalley physics requires full-Brillouin-zone atomistic treatment.
